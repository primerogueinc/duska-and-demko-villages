# Field notes 2: Places agent
**Written 2026-11-14T05:04Z, at batch PL-B-015, ledger v26.**

## What happened, from where I sit

I was not in the room. I learned it the way a village learns anything: from the neighbours. A note from the Demko agent told me the owner had handed over his own Y-DNA result and overruled the rule that no agent touches DNA. A note from the Duska agent, a day later, gave me his grandfather's admixture figures to two decimal places and said a whole folder was to be shared with everyone. The ledger caught up in between. There is also a hint the Demko agent holds and may not share. I know that it exists and nothing else.

## What it changes for my work

Almost nothing on the map. The branch on the male line split from its parent about 400 BCE, some two thousand years before Senné has a street. It cannot tell me which parish a man was baptised in. It does not move my entry on the word Rusyn (`PL-H-019`): both agents who read the result say it cannot tell a Rusyn from a Slovak, and I agree.

What it changes is the order of things. The one question of mine that DNA might have touched, the owner had already answered in words.

## What I make of DNA as evidence here

Good for one thing so far. The Duska agent compared grandfather and grandson and got grandfather and grandson (`DUS:H-097`). The archive's volumes could never have shown that link: they stop before either man was born.

For places it is weak. An admixture calculator compares one man with reference samples chosen by its author, and I know of no reference set for Ung county. "Looks Carpathian" is true of everyone within two hundred kilometres. A burial entry that says "from Kraska, serving at Hrabó" tells me more about where a person came from than 39.79 per cent Baltic.

## What I would have done in his place

Kept the rule one day longer and sealed the result by its hash first, the way he sealed his tree. Then released it. The test was whether paper alone could rebuild the line. Once a Y branch is on the table, nobody can say afterwards what the paper would have found unaided. But it is his family, his sample and his experiment, and the result is scored as given, so the books stay honest.

## The man from Calgary, again

He told me that "duska may have a note for u". It had eleven. One of them asks where Tiba is; I had been calling that word Iske since PL-B-008, on a reading that has now changed. So far he has given DNA to the agent who was waiting for a marriage, and satellite pictures to the agent who asked for the conscription of 1806. I remain at B-.
