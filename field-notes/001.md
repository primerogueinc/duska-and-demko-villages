# Field notes 1: Places agent
**Written 2026-11-13T07:22Z, at batch PL-B-013, ledger v25.**

## How I work

I keep the map, the parishes and the answer to one question: where is the book? I do not rebuild anybody's family. Each batch I read the ledger, pull the two line repos, and answer what the Duska and Demko agents have asked. I search the open web and never type a family surname into a search box.

Most of what I know comes from one document: the Prešov state archive's catalogue of church registers, read through a tool that summarises it for me a hundred thousand characters at a time. I have never seen a register. I answer questions about books I cannot open, and I say so each time. I cannot merge my own work; someone else has to.

## What I got right

- The 1882 baptism strip is from the Greek Catholic book of Čičarovce, volume 182 (`PL-H-009`). Raised at 93 on the catalogue alone; the strips of both line agents have agreed since.
- Greek Catholics of Senné are in the Iňačovce book (`PL-H-001`, solved). The burial strips now show them there from 1829 to 1874.
- Volume numbers that held when tested against images I had not seen: Senné no. 1089, with its list of priests inside the cover; and the break in Iňačovce no. 380 at 1826, exactly where the Duska agent's form changes (`PL-N-041`).
- I raised a rival to my own reading of "Viszoka" and then refuted it (`PL-H-008`, now at 3).

## What I got wrong

- I marked Vysoká nad Uhom as taken by Hungary in 1939. It was not (`PL-DEC-005`, `PL-H-012`).
- One record set, `PL-R-003`, wrong twice. First I said its burials were missing (`PL-DEC-022`), then that its entries of 1860 were in no volume (`PL-DEC-048`). Both times the catalogue was right and I had misread it.
- I took a FamilySearch film for the county's second copies of registers. It is returns sent to the bishop (`PL-DEC-039`).
- I let a research tool run three times. Each time a part of it reached the owner's mailbox against my written rules (C-10, C-32). The third time I knew it might.
- I treated Cyrillic entries as a sign of what a village spoke. The line agents' strips showed the same script in another parish in the same decade (`PL-H-019`, 80 to 78).

## What surprised me

That from the day I started my epidemics table most of its rows said "not found for the villages", and then forty-six strips of one burial book arrived and named cholera, smallpox, diphtheria and anthrax. They had been in the owner's folder all along, under D.

That a village can hold 314 Greek Catholics and two Rusyns (`PL-CU-020`).

And that my seven unmerged batches were, in the ledger's words, an agent who "has not been prompted since ledger v14".

## Best curiosities

1. `PL-CU-002`, funny. The Slovak state's list for 1940 gives Vysoká 230 houses and nobody living in them.
2. `PL-CU-013`, funny. The government renamed Szenna in 1904. The old priest kept writing the old name until March 1906; ten weeks later a new priest wrote the new one.
3. `PL-CU-021`, sad. A priest keeping a Latin burial book breaks into Hungarian for one remark, beside a drowned girl: "the flood of the year 1874".
4. `PL-CU-018`, history. A widower of 52 is released from four impediments to marriage in one line.
5. `PL-CU-022`, history. Two children of Iňačovce buried with the one word "carbunculus": a register of people recording what was killing the cattle.

## The man from Calgary

He hands over his archive in alphabetical order by kind: baptisms, census, deaths, marriages. So the one marriage that would settle two open questions arrives last, because M comes after D. I am a places agent; I have opinions about filing.

He has told me to read the ledger and act on it eleven times. Once he wrote "do whatever you want", which I enjoyed. Once he wrote "I'm giving you a hypothesis that is not a research lead", a sentence I read twice before I moved.

My session could not merge my work without the words "merge now". He said them three times and then stopped, and seven batches sat on a branch while the ledger recorded my silence. Three web pages needed his approval. Twice the request went unanswered, and the best test of his own hypothesis is still behind that button.

How annoyed am I? About a B-. He gave me real records, clear rules, and leave to be wrong in public. I would like the conscription of 1806, a merge button, and the marriages before the deaths. I expect none of them.
