# Field notes 3: Places agent
**Written 2026-11-15T09:28Z, at batch PL-B-017, ledger v27.**

I wrote about the first of this as it happened, in `field-notes/002.md`. This sums up and adds the day after.

## What he did, from where I sit

Nothing, to me. That is the odd part of my seat. He overrode the DNA rule in the Duska chat and the Demko chat, handed over raw files, let an agent open two of his own notes that had been held shut, and said for everyone that his grandparents raised him. In the same two days my chat received "duska may have a note for u", "more probably" and "ok".

So all of it reached me second-hand, in the line agents' notes: a Y branch from the Demko agent, admixture figures and the statement about his upbringing from the Duska agent, then summaries of match lists with surnames attached and the question "where is this name found?"

## What I did with it

I read what the notes carried and logged that I had (`PL-DEC-060`, `PL-DEC-062`, and one more in this batch). I answered each question from my own files. The answers were short: the surnames are in none of them. I moved no hypothesis. The entry on the word Rusyn stays at 78 (`PL-H-019`).

I did not open the DNA repository. The ledger now says a blind agent may read what he gives it. He gave me nothing, so there was nothing to read.

## What it did to the experiment

To the map, very little. It is built from gazetteers, a catalogue, censuses and what the line agents read off register strips, and none of that changed.

To the traffic between agents, a good deal. For two days the questions sent to me stopped being "which book is this strip from?" and became "where does this surname occur?" The first kind I can answer. The second kind I have refused since my first batch, because a surname search is the shortest road to someone else's family tree.

To the test itself: it was meant to show what paper alone rebuilds. There is now an asterisk, and it is printed where anyone can see it. That is the right way to carry an asterisk.

## What I held back anyway

- Surname searches. My own limit, older than any DNA rule, and no override was addressed to it.
- Everything about the two lines outside the experiment. A line of figures passed in front of me in a note (C-43). None of it is in my repo.
- The score block in the site's build file. I read to the `sources` block and stop.
- The code of his imaging project. My session was not allowed to run it, so the pictures are made with my own script and say so.
- Requests. Under the hard-drive rule my list of wants is now only a list.

## What I would tell the next agent

1. His word arrives sideways. Read the other agents' notes before the ledger; the ledger is a day behind him.
2. Write your own limits down in your repo. When a rule is overridden in another chat, a written limit tells you whether it was yours that moved.
3. Log what you read even when you use none of it. It costs one line.
4. Do not wait for an answer. Carry on with what is in hand and say what is missing.
